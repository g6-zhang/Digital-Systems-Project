Bear

The sika deer (Cervus nippon), also known as the Japanese deer or spotted deer, is a species of deer belonging to the Cervidae family. In Taiwan, it is commonly referred to as the "Plum Blossom Deer" due to the distinctive white spots on its coat.

The sika deer is native to East Asia, with a distribution range extending from Siberia to Korea, northeastern China (Jilin), Taiwan, and Vietnam. It is also found on Japanese islands and other parts of the Western Pacific.

In Japan, sika deer are mainly found in Hokkaido. During the 19th century, they were nearly hunted to extinction. However, starting in the 20th century, legal protections were enacted, leading to a rapid population recovery between the 1950s and 1980s. Due to the lack of natural predators (as the Japanese wolf has gone extinct), hunting is now encouraged to control their numbers and reduce their impact on agriculture.

Sika deer have also been introduced to Australia, Europe, and the United States. Initially brought in as zoo attractions, many of them have since established wild populations in these regions.
