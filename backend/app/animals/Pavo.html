The peacock is a general term for birds belonging to the order Galliformes and the family Phasianidae. It typically refers to the genus Pavo, which includes the Indian peafowl (Pavo cristatus) and the green peafowl (Pavo muticus), while in a broader sense, it also encompasses the Congo peafowl (Afropavo congensis), which belongs to a separate genus.

The male Indian peafowl has deep blue plumage with an iridescent metallic sheen and is found in India and Sri Lanka. The green peafowl, on the other hand, has bright green plumage and is native to Southeast Asia and Yunnan, China.Additionally, the Indian peafowl has two known color variations: the white peafowl and the black peafowl.

Peafowls exhibit clear sexual dimorphism, with male peafowls (peacocks) possessing long upper tail coverts that form the spectacular fan display, while female peafowls (peahens) lack this feature.

The famous peacock tail display is not solely for attracting mates. In the wild, when a male peacock encounters a predator and has no opportunity to escape, it may suddenly turn toward the threat and fan its tail. The large, eye-like patterns on its feathers serve as a warning and intimidation signal, a common anti-predator strategy in nature.

During courtship, a peacock's tail display is not only meant to attract peahens, but also to demonstrate its ability to deter predators, signaling that it possesses strong and healthy genes.
