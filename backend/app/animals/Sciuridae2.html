The family Sciuridae (scientific name: Sciuridae) belongs to the order Rodentia (rodents) and includes the subfamily Sciurinae (tree squirrels) and the subfamily Xerinae (African ground squirrels). A defining characteristic of this family is their long, bushy tails.Sciuridae, along with its closest relatives, forms the suborder Sciuromorpha (squirrel-like rodents). Squirrels are widely distributed across all continents except Oceania and Antarctica.

Squirrels are generally small in size, ranging from the tiny African pygmy squirrel, which measures 7–10 cm in length and weighs around 10 grams, to the large marmots (groundhogs), which can grow 53–73 cm long and weigh 5–8 kg.Unlike flying squirrels, squirrels lack skin flaps (patagia) between their limbs. They have strong limbs with sharp, hooked claws, which help them climb and grasp objects. Females are slightly heavier than males.

Species in the genus Tamias (chipmunks) and genus Sciurus (tree squirrels) possess cheek pouches that allow them to store large amounts of food.Squirrels have long, dense, and bushy tails, which help with balance and thermoregulation. Their limbs and feet are relatively long, with hind legs longer than the forelegs. Their ears are well-developed, capable of folding forward to cover their eyes, and during winter, some species develop tufts of black fur at the tips of their ears.

Squirrels are primarily herbivorous, with their diet mainly consisting of seeds and nuts, but they also consume bird eggs and fruits such as cherries and strawberries.Some squirrel species include insects in their diet, and certain tropical species even migrate in search of insects. In some cases, they have been observed preying on young birds, such as chickadee nestlings, by carrying them away.
