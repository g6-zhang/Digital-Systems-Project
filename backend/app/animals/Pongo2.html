The genus Pongo, commonly known as the orangutan, great ape, or red ape, belongs to the family Hominidae in the order Primates. Orangutans are native to the tropical rainforests of Indonesia and Malaysia, and their name, "Orangutan," comes from Malay and Indonesian, meaning "person of the forest."

During the Pleistocene epoch, orangutans once ranged across Southeast Asia and southern China, but today they are restricted to parts of Borneo and Sumatra. Previously, orangutans were thought to be a single species, but in 1996, they were divided into two species: the Sumatran orangutan (Pongo abelii) and the Bornean orangutan (Pongo pygmaeus). In 2017, scientists identified a third species, the Tapanuli orangutan (Pongo tapanuliensis). Orangutans are the only surviving genus within the subfamily Ponginae, having diverged from other great apes (gorillas, chimpanzees, and humans) between 19.3 and 15.7 million years ago.

Orangutans are the most arboreal of all great apes, spending most of their time in trees. They have long forelimbs, short hindlimbs, and are covered in reddish-brown fur. Adult male orangutans weigh around 75 kg, while females weigh approximately 37 kg. Dominant males develop large cheek pads (flanges) and use long calls to attract females or intimidate rivals. Younger or subordinate males lack these prominent features and more closely resemble females.Orangutans are mostly solitary, with social bonds primarily existing between mothers and their offspring. They are frugivorous, primarily consuming fruit, but they also eat herbs, tree bark, honey, insects, and occasionally birds. Their lifespan can reach 30 years or more in the wild.

Orangutans are highly intelligent and known for their ability to use tools. They construct elaborate nests using branches and leaves. Scientists have extensively studied their learning abilities, and evidence suggests that different orangutan populations may have distinct cultures.Since the 18th century, orangutans have been featured in literature and art, often in social commentary works. The renowned primatologist Biruté Galdikas pioneered the study of orangutans. Captive breeding programs for orangutans began as early as the 19th century, and they remain one of the most researched and recognized primate species worldwide.
