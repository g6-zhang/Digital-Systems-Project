Penguins belong to the order Sphenisciformes and the family Spheniscidae and are a group of flightless birds.They primarily inhabit the Southern Hemisphere, with 19 known living species worldwide and two extinct species. While most penguins are found in Antarctica, some species in the genus Spheniscus, such as the Humboldt penguin, Magellanic penguin, and African penguin, inhabit warmer temperate regions at lower latitudes. The Galápagos penguin, in particular, is found closest to the equator. Only two species— the emperor penguin and the Adélie penguin— live exclusively in polar regions.

Penguins range in height from 40 to 130 cm and weigh between 1 and 45 kg. The smallest species is the little blue penguin, while the largest is the emperor penguin.The Chinese name "企鵝" comes from Cantonese, where "企" means "standing", referring to the penguin's upright posture.

Penguins are considered the most cold-resistant birds in the world. Their bodies are densely covered in feathers, and they have a thick layer of subcutaneous fat, measuring 2 to 3 cm thick, which provides excellent insulation. This specialized thermal adaptation allows them to thrive in freezing environments with ease.

The great auk, a now-extinct bird that once lived in the Arctic, was likely as cold-resistant as penguins. Due to their similar appearance, early European explorers mistakenly believed great auks and penguins were closely related. As a result, when great auks were first discovered, they were given the same name as penguins in European languages, despite being unrelated species.
