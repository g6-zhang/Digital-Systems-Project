The giant panda (scientific name: Ailuropoda melanoleuca) is a mammal belonging to the family Ursidae in the order Carnivora. It is endemic to China and is characterized by its distinct black-and-white fur coloration. Its primary habitat is the mountainous regions surrounding the Sichuan Basin in central-western China, as well as the Qinling Mountains in southern Shaanxi.

As of 2016, the global wild giant panda population was estimated at approximately 2,060 individuals. Later that year, the International Union for Conservation of Nature (IUCN) reclassified the giant panda’s conservation status from "Endangered" to "Vulnerable".

In 2021, China’s State Council released a white paper on biodiversity conservation, reporting that the wild giant panda population had increased from 1,114 individuals 40 years ago to 1,864 individuals, officially confirming its status change from "Endangered" to "Vulnerable." The giant panda is China’s national treasure and is often referred to as a "living fossil" due to its evolutionary significance.

With its black-and-white fur and adorable appearance, the giant panda has won the hearts of people worldwide and has a large global fan base. Since its founding in 1961, the World Wide Fund for Nature (WWF) has adopted the giant panda as its official logo, making it one of the most iconic symbols of wildlife conservation. Additionally, giant pandas play a crucial role in China’s diplomacy, serving as a gesture of goodwill in international relations. Political scientist Joseph Nye has even described the giant panda as a key pillar of China’s soft power, drawing a comparison to the British royal family in terms of global influence.
