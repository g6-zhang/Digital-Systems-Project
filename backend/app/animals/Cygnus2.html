The genus Cygnus, commonly known as swans, belongs to the family Anatidae within the order Anseriformes. Swans are the largest living birds in this order and are also referred to as "Hō" or "Bai Hō" in classical Chinese, with the common name "swan". Swans are closely related to geese and ducks and are classified together with geese in the subfamily Anserinae.

Swans typically form lifelong pair bonds, but "divorces" can occur, especially after failed breeding attempts. If a mate dies, the surviving swan will usually find a new partner. Each breeding season, swans lay between 2 and 10 eggs.

With their elegant posture, melodious calls, and faithful nature, swans have become symbols of purity, loyalty, and nobility in both Eastern and Western cultures, particularly with reference to northern swan species.
