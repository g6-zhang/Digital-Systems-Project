Elephants, commonly referred to as "elephants", belong to the family Elephantidae (scientific name: Elephantidae) and are the largest living land mammals. They are classified under the order Proboscidea.

Elephants are widely distributed across sub-Saharan Africa, South Asia, Southeast Asia, and China’s southwestern border regions, inhabiting tropical and subtropical environments.

Elephants have thick skin but sparse body hair. Their trunks, which are elongated, flexible, and tube-shaped, are formed by the fusion of the nose and upper lip. They possess large, long upper incisor teeth, commonly known as "tusks", which are composed of ivory.

Inside their mouths, elephants typically have three premolars and three molars on each side. Their diet mainly consists of high-fiber plant materials, such as tree leaves and grasses. Unlike most mammals, elephants' teeth grow in succession, meaning new molars replace old, worn-down ones. By around 60 years of age, when their sixth and final set of molars wears out, elephants may suffer from malnutrition and starvation. However, in captivity, if provided with soft, ground food, they can live beyond this natural limit.

Elephants are known to possess high cognitive abilities, displaying complex behaviors, problem-solving skills, and strong social bonds. They are among the most intelligent animals, capable of recognizing themselves in mirrors, demonstrating empathy, and even mourning their dead.
