Sloths (scientific name: Folivora), sometimes mistakenly referred to as "tree otters", are a group of mammals belonging to the superorder Xenarthra and the order Pilosa.

Sloths are notoriously slow-moving animals, traveling at a speed of 4 meters per minute (0.24 km/h) in trees and only 2 meters per minute (0.12 km/h) on the ground. Due to their sluggish movement, they are vulnerable to predators when on land. However, they are excellent swimmers, which allows them to escape danger when necessary.

There are six extant species of sloths, found in the tropical rainforests of Central and South America. Although two-toed and three-toed sloths appear similar, they have notable differences. Contrary to their names, all modern sloths have three toes on their hind limbs; the primary difference is that two-toed sloths have only two fingers on their forelimbs.

Sloths' coarse fur provides an ideal habitat for symbiotic green algae, which helps them camouflage in their environment. Interestingly, sloths sometimes eat the algae growing on their fur as a nutritional supplement. These algae also serve as a food source for sloth moths, some of which are exclusively found on sloths.

Some species of giant ground sloths, known as "ground sloths", are now extinct. Among them, the largest species, Megatherium, could grow up to 6 meters (20 feet) long, comparable in size to a modern African elephant.
