The rhinoceros (scientific name: Rhinocerotidae) is a mammal belonging to the order Perissodactyla (odd-toed ungulates). It is a herbivorous species primarily found in Africa and Southeast Asia. Rhinoceroses are the largest living odd-toed ungulates and the second-largest land mammals, surpassed only by elephants.

All rhinoceros species are characterized by their short legs, robust bodies, and thick, wrinkled skin. They have large and stocky builds, with body lengths ranging from 2.2 to 4.5 meters, shoulder heights between 1.2 and 2 meters, and weights of 2,800 to 3,000 kilograms. Their skin is rough and thick, forming folded plates around the shoulders and lower back. They have sparse, coarse hair, with some species being almost hairless. Their ears are oval-shaped, and they have large heads, short thick necks, and extended upper lips for foraging.

One of their most distinctive features is their solid horns, which can be single or double (though some female rhinos may lack horns). These horns originate from keratin in the dermis and can regrow if damaged. Rhinos lack canine teeth, have short, thin tails, and their bodies range in color from yellowish-brown to dark brown, black, or gray.

Except for the white rhinoceros, all species are on the brink of extinction. The Javan rhinoceros is the rarest, with an estimated population of only 50 individuals, while the black rhinoceros population is between 10,000 and 30,000 individuals.These African and Asian rhinos face severe threats from habitat destruction, illegal poaching for their horns (especially in East Asia), and trophy hunting by Western tourists. Conservation efforts are crucial to their survival.
